# Ten rider personas map Singapore's connectors

An offline Singapore leisure-cycling app should ship **ten personas**: the seven requested (nature/photo, foodie, architecture, heritage, tourist, family, quiet/fitness) plus **wildlife/birding, art and murals, and café hopper**. "Sunset/beach" works better as a sub-tag of nature/photo, and "night ride" works better as an `opening_hours`/lighting filter than as a separate persona. Each persona has a distinct ride shape. Photographers and heritage riders want slow, stop-heavy rides with safe bike parking near the viewpoint. Foodies plan around opening hours. Families need short, flat, car-free loops that end at water play. Fitness riders want long uninterrupted stretches with no dismount points. Every persona has at least one strong PCN cluster: the north-east Round Island Route (RIR) for photo and wildlife, the East Coast to Changi and Alexandra–Tiong Bahru–Singapore River corridors for food, the Marina Bay/Kallang Basin loop for architecture and tourists, the Rail Corridor plus the Bukit Timah stretch of the Coast-to-Coast (C2C) for heritage, and the Mandai/Ulu Sembawang/Lower Seletar edge for quiet riding. OpenStreetMap can supply the generic layers well (parks 932, playgrounds 2,464, shelters 2,103, places of worship 654). It is weakest exactly where personas are distinctive: architecture tags are essentially absent, `heritage=*` has 34 objects, and hawker centres are tagged inconsistently. Those layers need a curated overlay keyed to OSM IDs. Bundling the OSM extract makes the app a distributor of an ODbL database, so it needs visible attribution, ODbL licensing of the OSM-derived data, and the ability to supply that data or the means to rebuild it. **Only two coordinates in all the research are sourced; every other point must be geocoded from OneMap or data.gov.sg before shipping.** Several key facts conflict or have changed in 2026, including the Rail Corridor South end date, Henderson Waves cycling, and the Gardens by the Bay waterfront closure on the tourist loop.

## Ten personas, each defined by a different ride shape

The persona set should come from the shape of the ride, not just from the type of destination. Comparable apps support this split. AllTrails filters by attraction (Beach, Historic site, River, Views, Wildlife, Wild flowers) and separately by suitability (Kid, Stroller, Paved), and multiple filters combine with AND ([AllTrails Help](https://support.alltrails.com/hc/en-us/articles/37227964040852-How-to-use-filters-to-find-trails)). Strava's planning POIs are landmarks, photo spots, peaks and bike shops, and its usage POIs are water fountains, cafés and toilets ([BikeRadar](https://www.bikeradar.com/news/strava-points-of-interest)). The Singapore Tourism Board's "Passion Tribes" add Foodies, Explorers, Culture Shapers, Socialisers and Collectors ([Travel Weekly](https://www.travelweekly.com/Asia-Travel/Passion-priority-Singapore-campaign); [NAS archive](https://www.nas.gov.sg/archivesonline/data/pdfdoc/20180829001/Wave%202%20Press%20Release_29%20August.pdf)). Singapore cycling editorials already pair PCN rides with hawker stops ([LTA Connect](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/who_we_are/statistics_and_publications/Connect/recommended-cycling-food-spots.html)) and with heritage routes such as the Rail Corridor and Singapore River bridges ([Seth Lui](https://sethlui.com/cycling-routes-singapore-guide/)).

Three candidate extras were tested and folded into other personas. **Sunset/waterfront and beach** overlap almost entirely with nature/photo, and Singapore's 111 `natural=beach` objects work as a sub-tag. **Night rider/socialiser** maps to STB's Socialisers, but it reads more naturally as an "open late" and "lit path" filter across the foodie and quiet personas. **Shopping/collectors** fits leisure cycling poorly. Family also works better as a constraint than as a destination persona: AllTrails treats kid- and stroller-friendliness as suitability filters, not interests ([AllTrails Help](https://support.alltrails.com/hc/en-us/articles/37227964040852-How-to-use-filters-to-find-trails)).

For the offline overlay, each spot record should carry `persona[]`, `route` (nearest named PCN or RIR segment), `why`, `best_time`, `status` with `valid_until`, `ride_mode` (`ride`, `push`, `park_and_walk`), `source_url`, and `geo_status` (`sourced` or `geocode_required`). The tables below follow that shape. Status is as of 20 Nov 2026.

## The north-east RIR is the photo, wildlife and quiet-ride core

Sunrise and sunset orientation matter more for this persona than any other attribute. East-facing water spots (Changi Bay Point, Changi Boardwalk, East Coast Park, Lorong Halus, Punggol's Sunrise Bridge) work at dawn. Spots facing west or the Johor Strait (Rower's Bay, Lower Seletar, Yishun Dam, Jewel Bridge, Tanjong Rhu, Marina Barrage) work at dusk ([TheSmartLocal](https://thesmartlocal.com/read/sunrise-sunset-spots-singapore/)). The north-east RIR packs the most of these into one stretch, so a photographer can start at Lorong Halus at dawn and finish at Rower's Bay or Lower Seletar at dusk. Otter timing on the Kallang also overlaps golden hour, so one ride window serves the photo and wildlife personas.

The biggest operational issue is that the best viewpoints are often where riding is banned. NParks bans cycling on Punggol Waterway's Kelong, Wave and Adventure bridges and boardwalk, the Pasir Ris mangrove boardwalks, the Changi Beach Park and Coney Island boardwalks, and in all nature reserves ([NParks sharing PCN, upd. 6 Oct 2026](https://pcn.nparks.gov.sg/sharingpcn/)). Two 2026 closures also hit this persona. **Punggol Point Park is closed from 11 Aug 2026 to about 31 Dec 2027, though its toilets stay open** ([NParks closure sign](https://pcn.nparks.gov.sg/files/6_Jul_2026_Punggol_Point_Park___park_closure_sign__003_.pdf)). At **Sembawang Park, the jetty section is closed to 31 Dec 2026 and the lawn section from 9 Sep 2026 to 30 Jun 2027** ([NParks](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/visit/parks/sembawang-park)).

### Quiet / fitness stretches

The quiet persona wants the opposite of the photographer: fewer stops, not more. The northern forest-edge connectors are the quietest and hilliest. Long rides suit the 75 km RIR eastern half, which opened 22 Jan 2022 with the full ~150 km loop due by 2035 ([NParks](https://nparks.gov.sg/news/2022/1/nparks-completes-phase-one-of-the-round-island-route-with-75km-of-recreational-connection-stretching-across-eastern-half-of-singapore)), and the 18 km Eastern Corridor ([NParks](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/news/news-detail/completion-of-the-eastern-corridor)). Speed is capped at **25 km/h on shared paths and 10 km/h on footpaths**, with radar and CCTV enforcement ([MOT, Feb 2026](https://www.mot.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/measures-to-prevent-speeding-and-reckless-riding-by-cyclists/)). "Quietest" rests on blog descriptions only, because no Strava heatmap data was retrieved.

## Marina Bay serves architecture and tourists; the Rail Corridor carries heritage

Architecture riders and first-time visitors want the same loop. The Marina Bay/Kallang Basin route passes about eight landmarks in quick succession on flat ground, and TheSmartLocal puts the Marina Bay Loop at about **11–11.5 km**, a realistic 2–3 hour leisure ride ([TheSmartLocal](https://thesmartlocal.com/read/cycling-routes-in-singapore/)). That figure is editorial, not official. The loop has a new 2026 complication: the **Gardens by the Bay Waterfront Promenade has been closed to cyclists since 4 May 2026, with no end date**, and riders detour around the Bay South perimeter ([LittleBigRedDot](https://littlebigreddot.com/gardens-by-the-bay-cycling-diversion-4-may-2026/); [GBTB diversion map](https://www.gardensbythebay.com.sg/content/dam/gbb-2021/image/about-us/media-room/2026/wetlands-by-the-bay/Diversion-map.pdf)). Route descriptions from 2021–2024 that ride past the Supertrees along the water are out of date. Expect event closures too: cycling was banned on the Helix and Jubilee bridges around Marina Bay on New Year's Eve 2024 ([Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/singapore/news/cycling-around-marina-bay-is-banned-on-new-years-eve-check-the-specific-locations-here-123024)).

Heritage riders get the strongest story line from the railway and WWII. The Rail Corridor and the Bukit Timah stretch of the C2C link the 1932 station, the truss bridge and the old fire station. The Southern Ridges and Labrador carry WWII, and Kranji covers Allied war graves. Heritage tags should distinguish **"NHB trail"** (Woodlands, Civic District, Bukit Timah), **"NHB museum"** (Bukit Chandu) and **"URA conserved building"** (Bukit Timah Railway Station), because these are different official statuses ([Roots trails](https://www.roots.gov.sg/nhb/trails); [URA PR21-05](https://www.ura.gov.sg/news/media/pr21-05/)).

### Tourist spots

The tourist persona has two realistic 2–3 hour rides: the Marina Bay loop from late afternoon into sunset, or an out-and-back on the East Coast Park seafront. Pulau Ubin and the full RIR east are day trips. The bumboat fare is commonly listed at S$4, but a September 2025 report describes S$5, so NParks advises confirming with the operator ([Mothership](https://mothership.sg/2025/09/man-who-refused-to-pay-increased-s5-pulau-ubin-boat-fare-stranded-for-an-hour-charters-another-boat-for-s48/); [NParks Ubin](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/pulau-ubin/visit/directions-and-map)). The two licensed bike-share operators in 2026 are **Anywheel and HelloRide** ([CCS](https://www.ccs.gov.sg/media-and-events/newsroom/announcements-and-media-releases/ccs-warns-hellobikesg-pte--ltd--for-attempting-to-propose-price-discussions-with-competitor/)). Their bikes are not foldable, so tourists cannot take them on the MRT; only folded bikes no larger than 120×70×40 cm are allowed ([LTA PDF](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/dam/ltagov/getting_around/active_mobility/pdf/foldable_bike_and_pmds_on_public_transport.pdf)). Helmets are mandatory on roads, and cycling on pedestrian-only paths has been an offence since 1 Jul 2025, with penalties up to S$2,000 and/or 3 months' jail ([LTA rules](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltaweb/en/walk-cycle-ride/rules-and-code-of-conduct.html)).

## Name the dish first; Bib Gourmand 2026 stalls are the safe names

The freshness filter for naming stalls is the **Michelin Bib Gourmand Singapore 2026 list: 97 entries, 10 new, announced 28 Jul 2026**. Seventeen establishments have held a Bib in all 10 editions, including Tian Tian and Tiong Bahru Hainanese Boneless Chicken Rice ([Eatbook 2026](https://eatbook.sg/michelin-bib-gourmand-2026/); [Her World](https://www.herworld.com/life/michelin-guide-singapore-adds-10-new-bib-gourmand-picks-2026)). The robust design is to recommend at the dish level ("chicken rice at Tiong Bahru Market") and attach the stall as an optional "famous pick" with a `bib_year`, then re-check each July. The 10 new 2026 entries are not placed below because no source gave their hawker centres.

## OSM covers generic layers well and persona signals poorly

Live Overpass counts taken on 20 Nov 2026 show that OpenStreetMap can generate every persona's generic layer, but not the layers that make a persona distinctive ([Overpass API](https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter); data © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL). **`building:architecture=*` has 0 objects in Singapore and `architect=*` has 16**, so architecture must be hand-curated. `heritage=*` has 34 objects, far fewer than Singapore's gazetted national monuments. Hawker centres are split across tags: of 171 objects named "Hawker Centre" or "Food Centre", 89 are `amenity=food_court`, **26 are `amenity=restaurant`**, 6 are `marketplace`, about 44 have no amenity, and many of those are **bus stops named after the centre** ("Opposite Newton Food Centre"). PCN route relations exist (`route=bicycle, network=rcn, ref=PCN`), but the Singapore wiki says the scheme is still a proposal, so do not assume completeness ([OSM Wiki: Singapore](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Singapore)).

All queries use the header `[out:json][timeout:120]; area["ISO3166-1"="SG"][admin_level=2]->.sg;` and end with `out center tags;` so that areas return one point. For "near the PCN", build `way(area.sg)[highway=cycleway]->.pcn;` and replace `(area.sg)` with `(around.pcn:300)`.

| heritage | `historic=*`; `historic=memorial`; `heritage=*`; `tourism=museum`; `amenity=place_of_worship`+`religion` | historic 377; memorial 111; heritage 34; museum 77; place_of_worship 654 (602 with religion) | `nwr(area.sg)[historic][name]; nwr(area.sg)[tourism=museum]; nwr(area.sg)[amenity=place_of_worship][religion];` | Monuments are not systematically tagged. `historic=memorial` includes small plaques, so filter `[name]` and `memorial!=plaque`. Water play is badly undercounted and needs curation |
| quiet_fitness / all (rest stops) | `amenity=toilets\|drinking_water\|shelter\|bicycle_repair_station\|bicycle_parking\|bicycle_rental`; `man_made=water_tap` | toilets 898; drinking_water 226; water_tap 23; shelter 2,103; repair 13; parking 741; rental 157 | `nwr(area.sg)[amenity~"^(toilets\|drinking_water\|shelter\|bicycle_repair_station)$"][shelter_type!=public_transport];` | Exclude bus shelters. `bicycle_rental` includes noisy dockless zones, so require a name or operator. Toilets inside malls, MRT stations and hawker centres are often unmapped; treat those as implicit rest stops |

Do not hit the public Overpass server from a build pipeline. It returned repeated **502 "at capacity"** errors during this research, and the operators recommend a local instance for automated use ([overpass-api.de](https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter)). The practical source is Geofabrik's Malaysia–Singapore–Brunei extract: a ~242 MB `.osm.pbf`, updated roughly daily, ODbL 1.0, with no Singapore-only file ([Geofabrik](https://download.geofabrik.de/asia/malaysia-singapore-brunei.html)). A suggested (untested) pipeline: clip with `osmium extract -p singapore.poly`, filter with `osmium tags-filter` on the tag list above, convert area features to centre points, buffer around the PCN, and write SQLite or JSON into the app bundle.

### ODbL obligations for the bundled extract

Bundling a filtered OSM extract means distributing a database, and the obligations attach to the data, not to the app code ([OSMF Licence FAQ](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Licence_and_Legal_FAQ)).

| Obligation | What it means in the app | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Attribute | Name "OpenStreetMap" and make clear the data is ODbL; "© OpenStreetMap contributors" with a link to openstreetmap.org/copyright works | [OSMF Attribution Guidelines](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Attribution_Guidelines) |
| Placement | Map corner, beside the map, or a splash screen. It may fade after interaction or 5 seconds, but must stay findable via an "(i)" button or About screen | [OSMF Attribution Guidelines](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Attribution_Guidelines) |
| Distributed database | Put attribution and the ODbL text or link in a readme or in the data's metadata | [OSMF Attribution Guidelines](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Attribution_Guidelines) |
| Share-alike | A derivative database (data you "adapt, modify, enhance, correct or extend") that you make available must stay ODbL | [OSMF Licence FAQ](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Licence_and_Legal_FAQ) |
| Offer on request | For produced works and derivative databases, offer the data or "the means to create" it. Publishing the osmium filter script satisfies "means" cheaply | [OSMF Licence FAQ](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Licence_and_Legal_FAQ) |
| Keep curated data separate | Personas, blurbs and Bib stalls kept in their own table keyed by OSM ID resemble the "collective database" pattern, where share-alike does not reach independent data | [OSMF Licence FAQ](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Licence_and_Legal_FAQ) |

Two grey areas need legal review. Persona scores computed from OSM tags could count as "enhancing" OSM data and so fall under share-alike. Merging the NEA/data.gov.sg hawker list, which is under the Singapore Open Data Licence, into the same rows as OSM data could create a derivative database. Keeping the layers separate and joining them only at display time is the safer pattern. The OSMF "substantial" and collective-database guidelines, which might exempt a small POI extract, were not reviewed.

## Every coordinate needs geocoding, and nine conflicts need a decision

The weakest layer in the dataset is location. **No research note sourced a lat/lon except two NParks "Navigate here" values (Sembawang Park 1.4615, 103.8368; Woodlands Waterfront Park 1.4527, 103.7801)**. Every other spot is a written description and must be geocoded from OneMap or the data.gov.sg PCN datasets before shipping ([data.gov.sg PCN loop](https://data.gov.sg/dataset/park-connector-loop)). Proximity under 300 m is unverified for The Interlace, the Former Ford Factory, Sultan Mosque, Kampong Lorong Buangkok and the Pinnacle@Duxton. More than half of the hawker centres' PCN links, and the stall-to-centre links for Margaret Drive Sin Kee, Tian Tian at Maxwell and Singapore Fried Hokkien Mee at Punggol Coast, are `[unverified-loc]`. The app should compute distance to the connector from geometry rather than trust these labels.

## Conclusion

The persona model works best as two orthogonal axes rather than ten flat categories. Destination interests (photo, wildlife, food, architecture, heritage, art, café) pick the spots. Ride constraints (family, quiet/fitness, tourist rules, night lighting) pick the segments. That split matches how AllTrails separates attractions from suitability, and it lets a family rider who loves food get flat routes to Bib stalls without a combinatorial persona list. It also matches the data. OSM supplies most of the constraint layers (shelters, toilets, playgrounds, cycleways) well, while the interest layers that differentiate the app (architecture, heritage, hawker stalls, water play) depend on a small curated overlay. That overlay is cheap to build at roughly 100–150 records, and keeping it in a separate table keyed to OSM IDs is also the cleaner ODbL position.

The practical risk is not a missing persona but stale truth. In 2026 alone, the tourist loop lost its Gardens by the Bay waterfront segment, the photographer's Punggol Point Park closed, the Bib list dropped two stalls, and the Rail Corridor South closure gained a firm end date on one official page while lacking one on another. An offline app freezes whatever it ships. Every status should therefore carry a `valid_until` and a source, stalls should sit under dishes rather than replace them, and the release process should re-check NParks notices monthly, the NEA closure dataset quarterly, and the Bib list each July. Geocoding comes first: with two sourced coordinates out of well over a hundred spots, location accuracy, not persona design, is what will decide whether riders trust the overlay.
