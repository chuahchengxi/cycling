# Bike-share operators in Singapore (as of Nov 2026): licences, parking/return rules, pricing, tourist rules

## Which operators hold LTA bicycle-sharing licences in 2026? Fleet sizes, licence dates

### Takeaway
Two operators are licensed in 2026: **Anywheel** (the largest, up to about 35,000 bikes) and **HelloRide** (HelloBikeSG Pte Ltd, about 20,000 to 25,000 bikes after a 2026 approval). SG Bike left in April 2024. I found no evidence that GoCycling or any other new entrant has a dockless bike-share licence.

### Cited Findings
- SG Bike exited the market in April 2024 after losses, leaving Anywheel and HelloRide. — [Wikipedia: Cycling in Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Singapore) (citing SUTD); [SUTD 2024](https://sutd.edu.sg/About/happenings/News/2024/7/No-longer-eyesore-are-shared-bikes-viable-option)
- July 2025: the shared fleet totalled 55,000 bikes (up from 36,000 three years earlier). Anywheel had about 35,000 and HelloRide about 20,000. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Singapore) (citing CNA 18 Dec 2025)
- LTA renewed HelloRide's licence for up to 20,000 bikes for one year, valid until **30 June 2026**. The approval was based on its record in managing indiscriminate parking and educating users. — [People's Daily / Xinhua, 2 Jul 2025](https://en.people.cn/n3/2025/0702/c90000-20335079.html); [Stomp](https://www.stomp.sg/news/bike-sharing-company-helloride-expand-spore-fleet-20000)
- HelloRide says that "following regulatory approval" it has expanded to **25,000 bicycles island-wide**. Its CEO, Hayden Choo, said it applied for 5,000 more through LTA's quota allocation process. — [HelloRide Global](https://www.helloride-global.com/helloride-grows-to-25000-bicycles-across-singapore/); [Zag Daily](https://zagdaily.com/micromobility/exclusive-helloride-reports-approval-to-scale-to-25000-bikes-in-singapore/). The approval date is unclear; one search aggregator said 13 July 2026, but I could not verify that.
- Anywheel's licence was renewed, with its fleet expanded by 5,000 to **35,000**. The date of that approval is unclear, possibly May 2025. — search result summary of Anywheel coverage (date unverified); 35,000 is also cited by [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Singapore)
- A Zaobao report (cited on Wikipedia) says the total fleet grew by another 5,000 around 1 July 2026. Wikipedia's sentence on this is garbled. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Singapore)
- HelloRide history: in July 2022 it received a Type 2 (sandbox) licence for up to 1,000 bikes. At that time Anywheel and SG Bike held Type 1 (full) licences. — [LTA, Jul 2022](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/newsroom/2022/7/news-releases/LTA_awards_bicycle-sharing_licence.html)
- Licence framework (LTA page updated 24 Nov 2025):
  - A Type 1 (full) licence lasts up to 2 years. Renewal must be applied for at least 3 months before expiry, with a $1,500 fee.
  - Fleet increases must be applied for at least 9 months before expiry, with a $750 fee.
  - LTA weighs the operator's plans for indiscriminate parking, fleet utilisation and parking-space availability.
  - [LTA Shared Mobility Services Licensing](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/industry_innovations/industry_matters/regulations_licensing/active_mobility/shared_mobility_services_licensing.html)
- Operator sanctions: fleet cuts, suspension or revocation, and financial penalties of up to $100,000 or 10% of annual turnover, whichever is higher. — [MOT written reply, 27 Feb 2026](https://www.mot.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/holding-bicycle-sharing-operators-accountable-for-bicycles-parked-outside-designated-zones-/)
- On 18 Aug 2026 (one source says 17 Aug), the Competition and Consumer Commission warned HelloBikeSG (HelloRide) for proposing price discussions with Anywheel in July and October 2025. Anywheel did not pursue either proposal. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Singapore) (citing the CCCS release)
- Other 2026 items:
  - A Ryde x HelloRide co-branded perk launched in June 2026. — [Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/ryde-partners-with-helloride-to-launch-first-last-mile-mobility-perks-in)
  - The SBS Transit app lets users reserve Anywheel bikes near bus stops (since Jul 2024). — [SBS Transit](https://www.sbstransit.com.sg/news/sbs-transit-and-anywheel-ink-partnership-to-enhance-first-and-last-mile-connectivity)

### Inferences
- HelloRide's 20,000-bike licence ran to 30 Jun 2026, and the 25,000 announcement came after it. That points to a mid-2026 renewal with expansion. Current total fleet is likely about 60,000 (about 35k Anywheel plus about 25k HelloRide), but this is not confirmed by LTA.
- The landscape is a duopoly. Any app feature that lists "operators" only needs Anywheel and HelloRide.

### Gaps
- No LTA press release for the 2026 licence renewals was found, so exact current licence expiry dates are unknown for both operators.
- GoCycling and other entrants: no evidence of an LTA dockless bike-share licence found. "Not found."

## How "end trip only at designated parking" works; penalties; are parking locations published?

### Takeaway
Since 14 Jan 2019, a trip can only end at LTA's designated bicycle parking: public racks or yellow-box lots, each with an LTA QR code, which riders scan in the operator's app. Parking elsewhere costs a **$5 fee** charged by the operator, and repeat offenders are **banned**. LTA's rack locations are public open data (data.gov.sg GeoJSON and a DataMall Bicycle Parking API). Operator-specific in-app zones are not published.

### Cited Findings
- QR-code parking system took effect **14 Jan 2019**. Users must scan the QR code at the parking location to prove proper parking before ending a trip. Bikes go in government bicycle parking zones demarcated by yellow lines. — [LTA Dec 2018](https://lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/newsroom/2018/12/2/qr-code-parking-system-for-bicycle-sharing-to-take-effect-on-14-january-2019.html); [Land Transport Guru](https://landtransportguru.net/bike-sharing-in-singapore/)
- Operators must integrate LTA's QR-code parking system into their apps and remove indiscriminately parked bikes within a stipulated timeframe. Users who park improperly pay a $5 fee, and repeat offenders are banned. — [MOT, 27 Feb 2026](https://www.mot.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/holding-bicycle-sharing-operators-accountable-for-bicycles-parked-outside-designated-zones-/)
- Original rule (2018/19): three or more indiscriminate-parking offences in a calendar year means a one-month ban from **all** bike-share services. Repeat offenders face bans of up to one year. — [Land Transport Guru](https://landtransportguru.net/bike-sharing-in-singapore/) (pre-2020 info; I could not confirm whether the current ban thresholds are unchanged)
- Enforcement stats (MOT written reply, **8 Sep 2026**):
  - About 95% of trips ended at designated parking (Jan 2025 to Jul 2026).
  - About 12,000 complaints in 2025, and about 15,000 in Jan to Jul 2026.
  - $5 penalty fees: about 90,000 in 2025, and about 148,000 in Jan to Jul 2026.
  - Users banned: about 4,800 in 2025, and about 4,800 in Jan to Jul 2026.
  - [MOT](https://www.mot.gov.sg/news-resources/newsroom/complaints-and-fines-related-to-shared-bicycles-parked-outside-designated-zones/)
- About 280,000 public bicycle parking lots exist at MRT stations, bus stops, HDB blocks and parks. Shared bikes must park at lots with a QR code. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Singapore) (citing LTA; date unclear). A 2018 figure was 207,000. — [LTA 2018](https://lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/newsroom/2018/9/2/lta-makes-bicycle-parking-neater-and-more-accessible.html)
- Anywheel also has private dedicated bays (e.g. Hotel JEN, Mandai Wildlife Reserve). Only Anywheel bikes can use them, and only the Anywheel app recognises their QR codes. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Singapore) (citing an Anywheel LinkedIn post)
- Trip.com tourist guides (7 Apr 2026) tell users to park only in "designated parking zones marked within the app to avoid penalties". — [Trip.com Anywheel](https://sg.trip.com/guide/phone/anywheel-singapore.html); [Trip.com HelloRide](https://sg.trip.com/guide/phone/helloride-singapore.html)
- A user review reports being charged $5 even when parked at a "P" sign, and being penalised because of a peeled ID label. This is anecdotal. — [Trustpilot](https://nz.trustpilot.com/review/www.anywheel.sg)
- **Published locations, offline use:**
  - data.gov.sg hosts the LTA **"Bicycle Rack" GeoJSON** dataset, last updated 6 Jun 2024. Its attributes are name and description only. Free download; rate limits are enforced from 31 Dec 2025 unless you sign up. — [data.gov.sg](https://data.gov.sg/datasets/d_937424cca6d1617288a82a7aeb89f76d/view)
  - LTA DataMall lists a dynamic **"Bicycle Parking"** API that returns bicycle parking locations within a radius of a point. It needs a free DataMall Account Key and is used under the Singapore Open Data Licence. — [DataMall Dynamic Datasets](https://datamall.lta.gov.sg/content/datamall/en/dynamic-data.html)

### Inferences
- For offline use, the data.gov.sg GeoJSON can be bundled in an app under the Singapore Open Data Licence. DataMall's API can top it up, but it needs a key, and the key should not be shipped in a client app.
- Not every public rack necessarily has an LTA QR code, so the GeoJSON is a superset or approximation of valid end-trip points. The operator app remains the authority on where a trip can end.
- "Geofence" in practice means the app checks GPS plus the QR scan against LTA parking points. No source documented a polygon geofence beyond that.

### Gaps
- Whether the rack datasets flag which racks carry LTA bike-share QR codes: not found.
- Operator in-app parking zones (including private bays such as Hotel JEN and Mandai) are not published as open data. "Not found."
- The current ban threshold (number of offences or length of ban) as of 2026 is not confirmed; the 2019 rule is cited above.
- The exact DataMall endpoint name and fields were not verified; check the DataMall API user guide PDF.

## Can a tourist sign up with a foreign phone number or card? Pricing

### Takeaway
Both operators work through the app, with no deposit for most users. The pay-per-ride base rate is about S$1 per 30 minutes. Anywheel sells passes (7-day S$6.90, 30-day S$9.90, 90-day S$26.90 as of Apr to Jun 2026). Foreign-number sign-up is reported only by a third-party guide, for HelloRide.

### Cited Findings
- **Anywheel** (Trip.com guide, 7 Apr 2026):
  - Regular bike S$1.00 per 30 min; family bike S$4.00 per 30 min.
  - Passes (unlimited rides under 30 min): 7-day S$6.90, 30-day S$9.90, 90-day S$26.90.
  - No deposit, but a minimum wallet balance is required (amount not stated).
  - [Trip.com](https://sg.trip.com/guide/phone/anywheel-singapore.html)
- Anywheel's 30-day pass is also sold via UPlay. Rides past the 30-minute limit are charged S$0.50 per extra 30 min. — [UPlay](https://uplay.com.sg/products/30-day-anywheel-bike-pass)
- Charges are in 30-minute blocks, so a 40-minute ride counts as two blocks (Sept 2026 guide). — search summary of [themoneybees](https://themoneybees.co/blog/bicycle-rental-singapore) (secondary)
- Older 2025 pass prices for Anywheel: 7-day S$3.90, 30-day S$9.90, 90-day S$23. — [SingSaver 2025](https://www.singsaver.com.sg/blog/bicycle-rental-singapore). This conflicts with the 2026 figures above.
- **HelloRide** (Trip.com, 7 Apr 2026): about S$1 per 30 min, plus about S$0.50 per 10 min after that. No passes are mentioned. — [Trip.com](https://sg.trip.com/guide/phone/helloride-singapore.html)
- HelloRide sign-up (Trip.com guide):
  - Enter an **international phone number** and receive an SMS code.
  - Pay by Visa, Mastercard, Apple Pay or Google Pay.
  - No mandatory deposit for most users. If one is required, it is refundable after 3 to 5 safe trips.
  - [Trip.com HelloRide guide](https://sg.trip.com/guide/transport/helloride.html). Note: this guide is partly China-focused, so treat it as unverified for Singapore.
- Anywheel tourist requirement: "valid mobile number and payment method". Foreign-number support is not stated. — [Trip.com](https://sg.trip.com/guide/phone/anywheel-singapore.html)
- Ryde integrated Alipay for international visitors (Ryde is a ride-hailing app that has a HelloRide tie-up). — [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/31451747/ryde-integrates-alipay-to-enhance-travel-convenience-for-international-visitors-in-singapore)

### Inferences
- A tourist with roaming SMS and a Visa or Mastercard can very likely use HelloRide. Anywheel foreign-number support is plausible but unconfirmed.
- 30-minute block billing means apps should warn riders near the 30-minute boundary.

### Gaps
- Official operator FAQs (anywheel.sg, HelloRide SG) were not fetched, so prices are from third-party guides dated Apr to Sep 2026 and may be out of date.
- Whether Anywheel accepts foreign numbers or foreign cards: not found.
- HelloRide pass prices in Singapore: not found.

## Rules: helmets, where bikes are banned, speed limits, dismount zones, Sentosa / Pulau Ubin

### Takeaway
- Helmets are mandatory on roads only.
- Bikes are banned on expressways, road tunnels, some viaducts, and (since 1 Jul 2025) pedestrian-only paths beside cycling paths.
- Riders must dismount at "No Riding" signs.
- Shared bikes are **banned on Pulau Ubin**.

### Cited Findings
- Helmets are required when cycling on roads. The LTA page does not require them on paths. — [LTA Rules & Code of Conduct](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/getting_around/active_mobility/rules_and_public_education/rules_and_code_of_conduct.html)
- Cycling is prohibited on expressways, road tunnels and selected viaducts; LTA links a PDF list. — [LTA](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/getting_around/active_mobility/rules_and_public_education/rules_and_code_of_conduct.html)
- Pedestrian-only paths: since **1 Jul 2025**, bicycles and PMDs are banned from pedestrian-only paths converted from footpaths beside cycling paths. Fine up to $2,000, up to 3 months' jail, or both. — [LTA factsheet Jul 2025](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/newsroom/2025/7/news-releases/pedestrian-only_paths_enforcement_starts_1Jul25.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Singapore) (citing ST 14 Aug 2024)
- Standalone footpaths and shared paths (marked with dotted red edge lines) remain shared with pedestrians. On roads, use bicycle lanes where they exist (Tanah Merah Coast Road; West Camp Road on Sundays 5am to 11am). — [LTA](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/getting_around/active_mobility/rules_and_public_education/rules_and_code_of_conduct.html)
- Other LTA rules:
  - Dismount and push at "No Riding" signs. Give way to pedestrians and walk the bike in crowded areas.
  - At night, use a white front light and a red rear light.
  - No phone use while riding.
  - On roads, groups are capped at 5 in single file or 10 two abreast.
  - [LTA](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/getting_around/active_mobility/rules_and_public_education/rules_and_code_of_conduct.html)
- Speed limits: 10 km/h on footpaths and 25 km/h on cycling and shared paths (2026 secondary guide). — search summary, no primary source fetched. This conflicts with the original 2018 limits of 15 km/h on footpaths and 25 km/h on PCN and shared paths. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Singapore) (citing ST 1 May 2018). The LTA page defers speed limits to its "Where You Can Ride" PDF.
- LTA runs 60 active-mobility enforcement cameras island-wide that target speeding (Aug 2026). — [LTA factsheet Aug 2026](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/newsroom/2026/8/news-releases/enforcement-operations-enhanced-active-mobility-regulations.html)
- Enhanced Active Mobility regulations took effect **1 Jun 2026**. They mainly cover PMAs, mobility scooters and fire safety, not manual bicycles. — [LTA Feb 2026](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/newsroom/2026/2/news-releases/tighter-active-mobility-regulations-from-1-jun-26.html)
- PCN paths are shared and marked with red continuous lines or "keep left" markings. The ICN shared paths have dotted red outlines; separated paths are red for cycling and grey for pedestrians. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Singapore) (citing NParks and LTA)
- **Pulau Ubin:** shared bicycles and PMDs may not be brought onto or ridden on the island (NParks notice updated 16 Jun 2026). Rent from the island's shops instead. — [NParks Pulau Ubin](https://pulau-ubin.nparks.gov.sg/notice-for-usage-of-bicycles-personal-mobility-devices-and-motorised-vehicles/)
- Sentosa has 4.5 km of on-road cycling lanes (2016). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_in_Singapore)

### Inferences
- A route planner for shared bikes should exclude expressways, tunnels and pedestrian-only paths, and avoid routing to Pulau Ubin with a shared bike.
- Riders using shared bikes on roads need their own helmet, since operators are not reported to supply one.

### Gaps
- Sentosa-specific rules for shared bikes (whether Anywheel or HelloRide can be ridden or ended on Sentosa, and where the parking zones are): not found.
- An authoritative current footpath speed limit (10 vs 15 km/h): the primary LTA PDF was not fetched.
- Fine amounts for bicycle speeding or riding on the wrong path, other than the pedestrian-only path penalty: not found on the LTA page.
- Whether operators restrict riding in Sentosa, Changi Airport or other zones through in-app geofences: not found.
