Notes on sourcing: All stall names below come from cited sources. Where a stall-to-hawker-centre link or a connector name is **not** stated in a fetched source and comes from general knowledge, it is tagged **[unverified-loc]** and should be checked before shipping. No coordinates are given; none were invented. "2026 Bib" means the stall appears on the 97-entry Michelin Bib Gourmand Singapore 2026 list (announced 28 Jul 2026) per [Eatbook 2026](https://eatbook.sg/michelin-bib-gourmand-2026/) and [Her World](https://www.herworld.com/life/michelin-guide-singapore-adds-10-new-bib-gourmand-picks-2026).

## Which hawker centres near connectors do food guides rate highest, and for which dishes?

### Takeaway
Cycling-specific guides from LTA and Yahoo/TheSmartLocal keep naming the same stops: Changi Village, East Coast Lagoon, Zion Riverside, Amoy Street, Margaret Drive, Pasir Ris Central and Old Airport Road. The densest clusters of Michelin-listed stalls are Newton, Hong Lim, Amoy Street, Tiong Bahru, Old Airport Road and Adam Road. The east coast (ECP to Changi) and the Alexandra / Rail Corridor / Singapore River side have the best mix of food and cycling.

### Inferences
- The strongest "foodie ride" corridors are (a) ECP to East Coast Lagoon to Changi Village and (b) the Alexandra / Queenstown leg of the RIR (Margaret Drive, Mei Ling, Alexandra Village, ABC Brickworks) going into Zion Riverside, Tiong Bahru and Hong Lim. Both are on cyclist-oriented guide lists and have Bib density.
- Changi Village and Zion Riverside are the only centres with explicitly cited cyclist-friendly bike parking. They are good defaults for a "bike-parking confirmed" badge.

### Gaps
- Bike parking is undocumented for most centres. The LTA article explicitly does not cover it.
- Exact PCN segment names for most centres are unverified (tagged above). NParks PCN maps or the OneMap API should be used to compute distance to the connector rather than these notes.
- Michelin's own "best hawker centres" page returned empty on fetch.
- No Ang Mo Kio stall with Bib status was found. Kebun Baru was not covered.

### Persona notes: what a cycling foodie wants and mid-ride vs post-ride dishes
Cited:
- Riders value bike parking at the table or at racks (Zion Riverside, Changi Village, Blue Balls) — [Yahoo 2023](https://sg.style.yahoo.com/11-pit-stops-recharge-along-120008493.html)
- Guides give an estimated ride time to each food stop, from 26 min to 2.5 h — [LTA 2025](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/who_we_are/statistics_and_publications/Connect/recommended-cycling-food-spots.html)
- Price anchors: most Bib hawker dishes cost S$2–12 (curry puff S$2, wonton noodles from S$4.50, fish porridge from S$6) — [Dollars&Sense](https://dollarsandsense.sg/most-represented-hawker-centres-in-the-michelin-bib-gourmand-2025-guide/)
- Timing matters. Chomp Chomp, Bedok 85 and many Lagoon stalls are evening-heavy, Tiong Bahru is morning-heavy, and Taman Jurong stalls sell out by evening — sources in the table above.

Inferences (not sourced, design guidance):
- **Mid-ride (light, quick, easy on the stomach):** curry puff (J2), shui kueh / chwee kueh (Jian Bo), soya beancurd / soy (House of Soya Bean, One Soy), fried banana, sugar cane, teh tarik / kopi, fish soup (Han Kee). Avoid heavy, oily or curry dishes.
- **Post-ride (heavy, rewarding):** Hokkien mee, char kway teow, satay with BBQ stingray and wings, nasi lemak, big prawn noodle, mutton soup, bak chor mee, chicken rice, duck rice.
- **Filters the app could offer:** open now / open at ETA, confirmed bike parking, Bib 2026 badge, distance off the connector, price band, and a "closed today (NEA cleaning)" warning.
- Shaded or covered seating is a likely priority given the heat, but no source documented shade per centre. This is a gap.
